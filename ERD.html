Read the relationships correctly

The symbol next to a parent PK tells how many parent records one child row can reference. The symbol next to a child FK tells how many children one parent can have. Nullable FKs allow zero or one parent; a non-null FK requires exactly one. A unique FK allows at most one child, otherwise zero or many. These are Crow’s Foot relationships, not DFD data-flow arrows. Each R-number identifies a foreign key in the register below.

Workflow constraints determine minimum child counts for completed transactions. Connector crossings do not create relationships. Every table and all of its fields appear once in this complete diagram.
